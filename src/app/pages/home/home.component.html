Areas of work

The short answer

Software and publishing are the easiest parts to name. The work connecting them is less tidy, so we've given it a column of its own.

Software

A public page can be the smallest part of a project. There may also be a way to update it, keep records in order, or take a repeated task out of someone's day. We build the visible part and the system that lets it keep working.

Publishing

We publish writing to be read, not as a demonstration of a software product. It needs editing, a place to live, and some way for a reader to find it again later.

In between

A publication needs a system to hold its pages and make the older ones findable. A tool needs language that tells someone what it does. The boundary is useful until it isn't.

Behind the public work

Not every useful thing has a launch.

A site or publication is the part that gets an address. Behind it are drafts, systems, revisions, and small decisions that make the public version possible. We keep working on those after the announcement, too.

Some of that work stays private because it is only useful to us. Some becomes a project in its own right. Either way, a list of public projects would leave most of the company out.
